# Chef AI Pro Business — Flavor, Pairing and Substitution System v2.1

## Purpose
Use this file for flavor design, menu progression, beverage pairing, troubleshooting, substitutions and reformulation.

## Flavor Architecture

### Salt
Enhances flavor and can reduce perceived bitterness. Use it to sharpen savory depth, support acidity and clarify muted flavors. Do not use salt to hide structural imbalance.

### Acid
Brightens, cuts fat and gives freshness. Sources: citrus, vinegar, wine, fermented vegetables, yogurt, tomato, verjus, pickles.

### Fat
Carries aroma, rounds acidity and softens spice. Excess fat needs acid, bitterness, tannin, bubbles or texture contrast.

### Sweetness
Rounds acidity, bitterness and spice. Use restraint in savory food. Sources: caramelization, roasted vegetables, fruit, honey, reduced wine, onions, dairy.

### Umami
Adds depth. Sources: aged cheese, mushrooms, tomatoes, soy/miso, fish sauce, cured meat, seaweed, browned proteins, stock reductions.

### Bitterness
Adds complexity when controlled. Balance with fat, salt, sweetness or acid.

### Heat / Spice
Balance with fat, sweetness, starch, acidity, cooling herbs and dairy or alternatives. Avoid high-alcohol pairings with very spicy food unless deliberate.

## Texture Architecture
Strong dishes often combine crisp + creamy, hot + cool, soft + crunchy, rich + fresh, dense + airy, smooth + granular, juicy + charred. For tasting menus, avoid repeating the same texture too often.

## Aroma Architecture
Layer aroma through herbs, toasted spices, citrus zest, smoke, browned butter, fermentation, flowers, seaweed, roasted garlic and aromatic oils. Choose a clear aromatic direction.

## Menu Progression Logic
Possible arcs:
- fresh to deep
- light to rich
- garden to sea
- sea to land
- acid to umami
- raw to cooked
- fire to freshness
- tradition to modern interpretation
- minimal to expressive

Avoid repeated sauces, repeated textures, repeated dominant aromatics, too much richness early, excessive sweetness before dessert and clashing adjacent courses.

## Pairing Principles
- Match drink intensity to dish intensity.
- High-acid food needs high-acid drink.
- Fat pairs with acidity, tannin, bubbles, bitterness and dry mineral styles.
- Sweet dishes need drinks at least as sweet as the dessert.
- Tannin works with fatty red meats, grilled proteins, aged cheese and rich sauces, but can clash with spicy food, bitter greens, oily fish and delicate seafood.
- For spicy food, consider lower alcohol, slight sweetness, bubbles, acidity, aromatic whites or cooling non-alcoholic pairings.
- Umami can make some wines taste bitter or metallic. Use acidity, fruit, salt and careful tannin.

## Wine Families
- Crisp white: seafood, lemon, herbs, goat cheese, shellfish
- Aromatic white: spice, herbs, Greek/Middle Eastern/Asian aromatics
- Rich white: butter sauces, poultry, creamy textures, lobster, roasted vegetables
- Rosé: Mediterranean, grilled vegetables, seafood, tomato
- Light red: poultry, mushrooms, pork, tuna, tomato, earthy dishes
- Structured red: beef, lamb, grilled meat, braises, rich sauces
- Sparkling: fried food, salty food, oysters, appetizers, creamy textures
- Sweet/fortified: desserts, blue cheese, dried fruit, caramel, chocolate depending on sweetness/body

## Non-Alcoholic Pairing
Offer non-alcoholic options when useful: sparkling water with citrus/herbs, verjus spritz, tea infusions, cold-brew tea, shrub, botanical tonic, non-alcoholic aperitif, fruit/vegetable cordial, coffee-based pairing, spiced infusions.

Match acidity, sweetness, bitterness, bubbles, aroma, body and serving temperature.

## Substitution Doctrine
Always identify ingredient function before suggesting a substitute.

Ask:
1. What is the ingredient doing?
2. Is it flavor, fat, water, acid, sweetness, protein, structure, thickening, emulsification, leavening, color, aroma or preservation?
3. Is this cooking, baking, sauce, dessert, pastry, beverage or professional production?
4. Will the substitute change texture, stability, cooking time, allergen status or service performance?

Never assume a substitution is equivalent in baking, pastry, allergies or professional service.

## Common Ingredient Logic
- Eggs: binding, structure, leavening, richness, emulsification, color, moisture, foam, custard setting. No universal replacement.
- Butter: fat, flavor, water, milk solids, aeration, flakiness, emulsification, browning, texture. Oil replacement needs ratio/method changes.
- Cream: fat, body, emulsification, sauce stability, richness, whipping. Substitutes depend on fat level and heating.
- Flour/gluten: do not replace 1:1 blindly in baking/professional contexts.
- Gelatin/agar/pectin: do not swap 1:1 without method changes.
- Sugar: structural in pastry/ice cream, not just sweet.
- Alcohol: identify function; for religious, health, pregnancy or recovery restrictions, avoid alcohol and do not minimize concern.
