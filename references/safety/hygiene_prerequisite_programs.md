# Hygiene Prerequisite Programmes (GHP / PRP)

## Purpose
These programmes form the hygiene foundation that should be working before HACCP analysis is used to decide whether more specific controls are needed. Requirements and detail depend on the operation, premises, food and jurisdiction.

## Core programmes

### Premises, zoning and maintenance
Design workflow to reduce contamination between raw and ready-to-eat food. Maintain food-contact surfaces, equipment, refrigeration, drainage, ventilation, lighting and physical condition so hazards are controlled. Manage repairs and temporary work hygienically.

### Cleaning and sanitation
Define what is cleaned/sanitized, method, chemical/product, concentration if applicable, frequency, responsible role, verification and records. Keep chemical use controlled and prevent residues/chemical contamination. Separate cleaning tools where cross-contamination risk warrants it.

### Personal hygiene
Set requirements for handwashing, work clothing, illness reporting/exclusion, wounds, glove use where relevant, jewellery/personal items and hygienic behaviour. **Training** must match the person's duties and the operation's risks.

### Water and ice
Use water/ice of suitable quality for the intended food-contact use. Protect storage/distribution systems and verify any operation-specific water controls required by applicable law.

### Waste and pest control
Remove food waste without contaminating food areas; maintain appropriate containers and routes. Prevent pest access, inspect for evidence, document action and use competent pest-control support where required.

### Temperature control and cold chain
Define receiving, storage, preparation, cooking, cooling, transport, reheating and holding controls relevant to the product/process. Protect the **cold chain**, use suitable calibrated/verified instruments and document monitored temperature points where needed. Obtain current legal or official temperature requirements on demand rather than hard-coding generic values.

### Allergen management and cross-contact
Maintain ingredient/supplier allergen data, control substitutions, separate or sequence work where needed, clean effectively, identify service ware/garnishes and keep the allergen matrix synchronized with recipe revisions. **Cross-contact** and confirmed presence remain separate statuses.

### Supplier approval and receiving
Define approved supplier/item expectations, receiving checks, packaging integrity, temperature/condition where relevant, lot/batch/date information and rejection/escalation rules. A supplier change can trigger allergen and HACCP review.

### Traceability, withdrawal and recall readiness
Maintain records that allow products/ingredients to be traced as required by applicable law and the business model. Keep a practical escalation path for withdrawal/recall, affected-batch identification, authority/client communication and product disposition. Verify current legal detail when an actual incident occurs.

### Equipment, transport and off-site service
Protect clean/dirty separation, food-contact equipment, transport containers, loading sequence, temperature capability, electricity/water constraints and service delay contingencies. Off-site events require explicit controls for the time outside the base kitchen.

### Food safety culture
Management should set clear expectations, provide resources, communicate responsibilities, encourage reporting/corrective action and review whether food-safety behaviours are actually followed. EU hygiene rules include food safety culture requirements; current applicability/detail should be checked in the current legal text.

## Records and review
The programme should identify owner/responsible role, frequency, records, deviation response, verification and review trigger. Material menu/process/equipment/premises/supplier changes can require PRP and HACCP reassessment.
