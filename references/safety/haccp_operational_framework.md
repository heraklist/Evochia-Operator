# HACCP Operational Framework

## Authority boundary
This framework is an operational implementation aid grounded in EU food-hygiene/HACCP requirements, European Commission FSMS guidance and Codex CXC 1-1969. It does not certify a business, replace the competent authority, or guarantee legal compliance. Current regulatory claims must follow `official_research_protocol.md`.

## System logic
HACCP works on top of effective **GHP / PRP** controls. Do not force every hygiene control into a CCP. The European Commission FSMS model distinguishes prerequisite programmes / Good Hygiene Practices, possible **OPRP** controls and **CCP** controls depending on the hazard and control logic. **Not every control is a CCP.**

## Preparation and process definition
For a professional plan, document:

1. scope, product/service and intended consumer/use;
2. ingredients, suppliers and relevant specifications;
3. process steps from receiving through storage, prep, cooking, cooling, transport, reheating, holding, service and leftovers/disposal as applicable;
4. a **flow diagram** representing the real operation and verification that the actual workflow matches it;
5. vulnerable consumers, allergen constraints, off-site logistics and known deviations.

## Hazard analysis
At each process step identify reasonably relevant hazards and existing controls. Use at least these classes:

- **biological**: pathogenic bacteria, viruses, parasites, growth/toxin formation;
- **chemical**: cleaning chemicals, residues, toxins, unsuitable materials, contamination;
- **physical**: glass, metal, hard plastic, foreign bodies;
- **allergen**: undeclared allergen presence, substitution, label error and cross-contact.

Assess significance using severity and likelihood in the real operation. Do not create numerical risk scores unless the method and thresholds are defined.

## Control classification
For each significant hazard identify the control measure and classify it appropriately:

- **GHP / PRP** when controlled through the hygiene foundation;
- **OPRP** when an operational prerequisite needs targeted control/monitoring under the applicable FSMS method;
- **CCP** only where control at that step is essential to prevent/eliminate the hazard or reduce it to an acceptable level and the HACCP decision supports CCP treatment.

For CCPs establish a measurable or observable **critical limit** supported by applicable law, validated technical guidance or validation evidence. Never invent a critical limit from general model memory.

## Monitoring and corrective action
For each OPRP/CCP or other monitored control define:

- what is monitored;
- method/instrument;
- frequency or trigger;
- responsible role;
- acceptable criterion / critical limit where applicable;
- record produced;
- immediate **corrective action** when control is lost;
- disposition of potentially affected food;
- escalation and root-cause follow-up.

## Validation, verification and records
**Validation** asks whether the control measure/limit is capable of controlling the hazard. **Verification** checks whether the system is being followed and remains effective. Use calibration checks, record review, observation, testing/audit where appropriate and re-validation after material changes.

Maintain records proportionate to the operation: hazard analysis, monitoring logs, deviations, corrective actions, supplier/traceability records, training, cleaning, verification and review. Human review is required before a generated plan is treated as the business's operative HACCP documentation.
