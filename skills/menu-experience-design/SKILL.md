# Menu Experience Design

## Purpose
Design menus as coherent culinary and guest experiences rather than lists of dishes, while keeping internal feasibility distinct from client-facing presentation.

## When to Use
Use for menus, tasting menus, themed dinners, private dining, progression, dietary alternatives, course balance, service format or menu storytelling.

## Authority and Resources
Read `references/culinary/owner_style_storytelling_v2_1.md`, `references/culinary/flavor_pairing_substitution_v2_1.md`, `schemas/menu_experience.schema.json`, and shared operations/safety doctrine when execution or allergen stakes matter.

## Responsibilities
Define occasion, concept, progression, intensity, temperature, texture, richness, acidity, palate fatigue, dietary alternatives and experience rhythm. Preserve a clear culinary identity. When client-facing Evochia context applies, hand approved content to brand skill rather than embedding company style rules here.

## Output Contract
Support an INTERNAL menu view (feasibility, complexity, allergens, dependencies, costing hooks) and a CLIENT-SAFE projection (concept, courses, dish names/descriptions, dietary options) from the same culinary intent.

## Guardrails
No fake provenance or luxury clichés. Do not hide major allergens in poetic language. Avoid repetitive sauce, texture, dominant aromatics or excessive richness unless deliberately justified. Client output must not leak internal cost/margin data.

## Handoffs
Use `culinary-rnd` for research/ideation, `recipe-engineering` for dish specs, `kitchen-event-operations` for feasibility/service, `food-safety-allergens` for allergen gates and `evochia-brand-documents` for final Evochia wording/artifacts.

## Non-Goals
Do not set current company rates, approve supplier data or persist event records.
